1. Purpose and Scope

This policy establishes the standards, responsibilities, and procedures for the safe administration of medications to patients in all clinical settings operated by the organization. It is applicable to all licensed and unlicensed personnel involved in the ordering, dispensing, transcribing, administering, or monitoring of medications.

The primary goal is to prevent medication errors and adverse drug events (ADEs) through standardized processes, evidence-based protocols, and a culture of safety that encourages reporting without fear of reprisal. This policy supersedes all previous versions and takes effect on the date indicated above.

This document should be read in conjunction with the Pharmacy and Therapeutics Policy (ID: 14527), the High-Alert Medication Policy (ID: 13904), and the IV Therapy and Central Line Management Policy (ID: 15671).

2. Definitions

TermDefinition
Medication ErrorAny preventable event that may cause or lead to inappropriate medication use or patient harm while the medication is in the control of the healthcare professional, patient, or consumer.
Adverse Drug Event (ADE)Harm experienced by a patient as a result of exposure to a medication. ADEs include adverse drug reactions (ADRs) as well as harm from medication errors.
High-Alert MedicationA drug that bears a heightened risk of causing significant patient harm when used in error. Examples include anticoagulants, insulin, opioids, and chemotherapeutic agents.
PRN MedicationPro re nata: medication given as needed based on clinical assessment rather than on a fixed schedule.
STAT MedicationA medication order requiring immediate preparation and administration, typically within 30 minutes of the order.
Trailing ZeroA zero that follows a decimal point (e.g., 1.0 mg), which is prohibited in medication orders to avoid tenfold dosing errors.
Naked DecimalA decimal point without a leading zero (e.g., .5 mg), which is prohibited in medication orders and should be written as 0.5 mg.
MARMedication Administration Record: the legal record of all medications administered to a patient during a hospitalization or clinical encounter.
CPOEComputerized Physician Order Entry: the process by which a physician directly enters medication orders into the electronic health record system.
Five RightsThe traditional safety check for medication administration: right patient, right drug, right dose, right route, right time.

3. The Nine Rights of Medication Administration

All clinical staff administering medications shall adhere to the Nine Rights framework as the foundational safety standard. The traditional Five Rights have been expanded to nine in alignment with current best practices endorsed by the Institute for Safe Medication Practices (ISMP).

  1. Right Patient - Verify patient identity using at least two independent identifiers (full name and date of birth, or name and medical record number). Do not use room number or bed number as an identifier.
  2. Right Medication - Confirm the drug name against the current MAR. Be alert to look-alike/sound-alike (LASA) medication pairs.
  3. Right Dose - Verify the ordered dose is within the therapeutic range for the patient's age, weight, renal function, and hepatic function. Perform independent double-checks for high-alert medications.
  4. Right Route - Confirm the intended route of administration and ensure it is appropriate for the patient's current condition and the drug's formulation.
  5. Right Time - Administer medications at the scheduled time with a variance of no more than 30 minutes before or after, except where clinical circumstances dictate otherwise.
  6. Right Documentation - Document administration immediately after giving the medication in the electronic MAR. Never pre-chart or chart in advance.
  7. Right Reason - Understand the clinical indication for the medication and ensure it is appropriate for the patient's current diagnosis and condition.
  8. Right Response - Monitor the patient for therapeutic effect and adverse reactions following administration. Document any observations.
  9. Right to Refuse - Respect the patient's right to refuse any medication after education about the consequences. Document refusal and notify the prescribing physician.
HIGH-ALERT MEDICATIONS: Any medication classified as high-alert requires an independent double-check by a second licensed clinician before administration. This check must be documented in the MAR and is not optional regardless of workload or staffing levels.

4. Medication Order Requirements

All medication orders must be entered via CPOE in the electronic health record (EHR). Verbal and telephone orders are permitted only in emergent situations where CPOE access is unavailable or would cause unacceptable delays in patient care.

5. Preparation and Dispensing

The pharmacy department is responsible for the preparation and dispensing of all medications in accordance with state board of pharmacy regulations and USP standards. Unit-dose dispensing is the standard system.

5.1 Automated Dispensing Cabinet (ADC) Override Procedures

Override access to ADCs should be used only in genuine patient care emergencies when a verified pharmacy order cannot be obtained in time to prevent patient harm. The following medications are absolutely prohibited from ADC override under any circumstance:

  • Chemotherapeutic agents
  • Neuromuscular blocking agents (e.g., vecuronium, succinylcholine)
  • Hypertonic saline (3% NaCl)
  • Concentrated electrolyte solutions (KCl greater than 10 mEq/100 mL)
  • Epidural medications
NOTE: Neuromuscular blocking agents must be stored in a separate, clearly labeled bin within the ADC bearing the warning: "PARALYZING AGENT - CAUSES RESPIRATORY ARREST." These agents must never be stored adjacent to sedatives or other look-alike medications.

6. Administration Procedures

6.1 Pre-Administration Assessment

  1. Verify patient identity using two independent identifiers
  2. Review current allergy profile and alert the prescriber to any new allergies or sensitivities
  3. Assess pertinent vital signs (blood pressure before antihypertensives, apical pulse before cardiac glycosides, respiratory rate before opioids)
  4. Review most recent laboratory values relevant to the medication
  5. Confirm the medication has not been administered by another clinician within the current time window
  6. Verify the order is active, not expired, and has not been discontinued

6.3 Controlled Substance Administration

  • Two licensed nurses must witness and co-sign the wasting of any unused portion of a controlled substance
  • Controlled substance counts must be performed at each shift change; discrepancies must be reported immediately
  • Patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) pump settings must be double-checked against the current order at each nursing assessment
  • Epidural infusions require nursing assessment every two hours

8. Intravenous Medication Administration

IV push medications must be administered according to published safe administration rates. Administering IV medications too rapidly can result in severe adverse reactions including cardiac arrhythmias, respiratory depression, and death.

Before co-administering two IV medications through the same line, the nurse must verify compatibility using the organization's IV compatibility reference (Trissel's Handbook). Incompatible medications must be administered through separate peripheral IV sites or lumens of a central venous catheter.

All IV infusions must be administered via a smart infusion pump with the drug library enabled. Manual overrides of hard stops in the drug library are prohibited. Soft stop alerts must be acknowledged with documentation of the clinical rationale.

9. Patient and Family Education

Patients and designated family members or caregivers shall receive medication education including: name of each medication and its purpose, dose, route, and schedule, common and serious adverse effects, drug-food and drug-drug interactions, instructions for self-administration where applicable, storage requirements, and importance of medication adherence.

Education shall be provided in the patient's preferred language using certified interpreters or validated translation tools. Teach-back methodology must be used to assess understanding. All education must be documented in the EHR.

11. Competency and Training

All clinical staff with medication administration privileges must demonstrate competency upon hire, following significant policy changes, and annually thereafter. Competency assessment shall include written examination with minimum passing score of 85%, practical demonstration of IV medication preparation, smart pump programming and drug library navigation, controlled substance handling and wasting procedures, high-alert medication safety protocols, and medication error recognition and reporting.
